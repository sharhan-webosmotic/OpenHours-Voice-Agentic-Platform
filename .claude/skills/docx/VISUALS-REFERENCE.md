## Visuals & Diagrams Guidelines

### Flowcharts, Architecture Diagrams, and Process Maps

- Ensure arrows, connectors, and relationship lines are clearly visible and do not overlap text, icons, or diagram elements.
- Maintain sufficient spacing between nodes to keep diagrams readable at standard document zoom levels.
- Use consistent directional flow (preferably left-to-right or top-to-bottom).
- Avoid crossing lines whenever possible.
- Keep labels concise and place them near the relevant connectors without obstructing the flow.
- Validate that all arrows correctly represent the intended process sequence and dependencies.

### Images and Visual Assets

- Use visuals to communicate information instead of repeating the same content in surrounding text.
- When a diagram, table, or image already explains a concept, provide only a brief supporting explanation rather than duplicating all details.
- Ensure each image adds unique value and is not included for decorative purposes alone.
- Position images close to the related content section.
- Use clear titles and captions where necessary.

### Content Optimization

- Avoid redundant explanations between text, diagrams, tables, and images.
- If information is effectively represented visually, summarize the key takeaway rather than restating the full content.
- Prioritize concise, high-impact documentation that balances visual communication with written explanations.
- Eliminate duplicate requirements, process descriptions, or workflow details that appear in both text and visuals.
- Optimize for readability, stakeholder review, and executive consumption.

### Quality Check Before Finalizing

- Verify all diagrams are readable without zooming excessively.
- Confirm arrows and connectors do not overlap text.
- Ensure images are properly aligned and scaled.
- Check that visuals and text complement each other rather than repeating the same information.
- Remove any redundant content to keep the document concise, professional, and impactful.
